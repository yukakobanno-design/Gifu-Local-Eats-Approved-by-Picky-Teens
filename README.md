Gifu Local Eats: Approved by Picky Teens! Welcome to our fun guide to Gifu's best local comfort foods!

Fun Fact from My Childhood:
During the COVID lockdown, I really wanted to eat Gohei Mochi, but it wasn't listed in the supermarket delivery catalog. So... I decided to make it myself!

I put the cooked rice into a large ceramic mixing bowl and pounded it with a rolling pin! Since I didn't have flat skewers, I used chopsticks instead.

That was the hilarious start of my Gohei Mochi adventure!　You can also find them at regular supermarkets, too!
<img width="1920" height="1818" alt="五平餅真空" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/eb587e7d-43f5-4b96-a619-03d47f02c907" />They're good cooked in a frying pan or a toaster oven, but if you've got charcoal, it's next-level amazing! Just slather on that sweet miso sauce and take a big bite!
<img width="1920" height="1440" alt="五平餅" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/0cc0556c-486d-40e0-916b-e0b18d98e830" />
In Gifu, there are so many little shops selling Gohei Mochi and Japanese sweets, and you can usually grab them at in-store shops inside supermarkets, too!　You can just grab them fresh from a local store, or bake them at home and pour that amazing sweet sauce right on top. So good!　To be honest, though, they can be pretty messy to eat! You almost always get some sweet sauce on your face, and it's a guarantee that at least one drop will end up on your clothes. (Worth it, though!)

Next up: Keichan! It’s a total local classic we eat all the time in Gifu. It’s made by marinating chopped chicken in a rich miso sauce and then stir-frying it together with veggies.For some reason, we always stir-fry it with tons of cabbage, usually in a rich miso flavor! The flavor is pretty strong and savory, but I'm totally addicted.
